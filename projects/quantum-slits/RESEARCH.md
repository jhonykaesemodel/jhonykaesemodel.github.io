# The Space Between — learning and evidence model

## Learning contract

- **Question:** Why does opening a second slit create places where single quantum events stop arriving?
- **Likely misconception:** A photon is a tiny ball secretly choosing a slit, while “the wave” is decorative water-like animation or many photons colliding with one another.
- **Irreducible interaction:** Build the one-slit distribution first, open the second slit, reveal the two phase fields adding and cancelling, then watch isolated events accumulate into that predicted pattern.
- **Transformation:** Before, the visitor sees mysterious waves and dots. After, they can reason that alternatives contribute amplitudes, amplitudes can cancel, and each experiment still ends in one localized event.
- **Boundary:** The detector distribution and amplitude addition are calculated. The 3D scalar surface, scale, color, camera, and apparatus proportions are explanatory. It is not a literal photon, water medium, full electromagnetic field, or time-dependent quantum-field simulation.

## Computed model

- One slit uses a Fraunhofer sinc² diffraction envelope.
- Two slits use that envelope multiplied by coherent two-path interference, with the Born-rule distribution normalized before event sampling.
- The wave surface adds scalar sinusoidal contributions based on distance from each opening. It preserves the crucial phase relationship but does not claim literal electromagnetic or wavefunction geometry.
- Perfect path marking uses zero fringe visibility. Interpretations remain optional lenses and do not alter the shared observations.
